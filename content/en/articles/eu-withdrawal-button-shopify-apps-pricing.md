## What has French law required since June 19, 2026?

Ordinance no. 2026-2 of January 5, 2026 transposes Directive (EU) 2023/2673. It amends article L. 221-21 of the French Consumer Code, and decree no. 2026-3 of the same day creates article D. 221-5. Despite their title about financial services, these texts cover all distance contracts concluded electronically, according to the law firms [Racine](https://www.racine.eu/entree-en-vigueur-des-nouvelles-regles-relatives-au-droit-de-retractation-pour-les-contrats-conclus-a-distance/) and [Lexing](https://www.lexing.law/avocats/bouton-de-retractation-ce-qui-change-pour-le-commerce/2026/03/30/).

In practice, according to these analyses and the one from the [Paris Île-de-France CCI](https://www.entreprises.cci-paris-idf.fr/actualites/e-commerce-et-droit-de-retractation-une-nouvelle-obligation-pour-les-professionnels):

1. A "renoncer au contrat ici" button or link (or an equivalent unambiguous phrase), visible, directly and easily accessible, available throughout the withdrawal period.
2. An online statement where the customer enters or confirms their first and last name, the order identification and the email address for the acknowledgement.
3. A second "confirmer la rétractation" (confirm withdrawal) button.
4. An acknowledgement of receipt on a durable medium, with the content of the statement and the date and time it was sent.

The obligation applies to contracts concluded from June 19, 2026, Racine notes. Your terms and conditions must also state that the function exists and where it is, according to [Village de la Justice](https://www.village-justice.com/articles/commerce-droit-retractation-ajout-obligatoire-une-fonctionnalite-retractation,55675.html). On penalties, Lexing and [UFC-Que Choisir](https://www.quechoisir.org/actualite-achats-en-ligne-les-sites-doivent-desormais-proposer-un-bouton-de-retractation-n177246/) cite an administrative fine of up to €15,000 (individual) or €75,000 (company).

## The rest of the withdrawal right does not change

The core rules remain those set out by [Service-Public](https://www.service-public.gouv.fr/particuliers/vosdroits/f10485) (page checked January 1, 2026):

| Rule | What Service-Public says |
| --- | --- |
| Period | 14 days from the day after the goods are received (the last item for an order shipped in several parcels) |
| Missing information | Period extended by 12 months if the seller did not inform the customer of the right |
| Return costs | Paid by the customer, unless you cover them or did not tell them they had to pay |
| Sending goods back | No later than 14 days after withdrawing |
| Refund | Within 14 days, delivery costs included, with the same payment method; you can wait until you get the goods back or proof of shipment |
| Late refund | 5% surcharge from 10 days late, then more depending on the delay |

The exceptions have not moved either: made-to-measure or personalized products, perishables, or unsealed items that cannot be returned for hygiene reasons.

## Which app fits your case?

### Withdrax: the most complete free plan

The **Withdrax** Free plan includes the button and form, unlimited requests, customer email confirmation, merchant notifications and automatic return creation in Shopify. The Pro plan ($4.99/month) adds order verification, approval or rejection emails and country-based visibility. Limits: the listing shows English only, and data retention is 3 months on Free.

### Withdrawly: free and in French

**Withdrawly** is the only free app that lists French. The Free plan offers a two-step form, an automatic acknowledgement of receipt, a withdrawal-period check and EU hosting. Custom email templates come with Essential ($9/month), the full audit log with Professional ($25/month).

### Easy Withdraw: the longest trial

**Easy Withdraw**, based in Berlin, covers the 24 EU languages, calculates the withdrawal period and generates signed PDFs. Its three plans ($5, $15 and $25/month) have a 90-day trial. Rejecting out-of-period requests and automatic order cancellation come with Pro. It is the listing with the most reviews (6).

### Compliantly and WithdrawKit: one paid plan each

**Compliantly** ($4.99/month) advertises a two-step confirmation, automatic confirmation emails, a central history and Excel export, with no customer login. **WithdrawKit** ($13.89/month) hosts the page on your domain, emails a timestamped receipt and produces PDF evidence packs. It also handles refunds and restocking.

### EUWithdraw and Lifetime: two different models

**EUWithdraw** adds an entry point through a theme block and records statements with timestamps; its Free plan shows no limits. **Lifetime EU Withdrawal Button** costs $299 once, but its emails go through the FlowSend app with usage-based fees not quantified on the listing.

## How to choose

- **Low volume, zero budget:** **Withdrax** if an English admin is fine, **Withdrawly** if you want French.
- **You sell in several EU countries:** **Easy Withdraw** or **Withdrawly**, which cover EU languages.
- **You want evidence in case of a dispute:** **WithdrawKit** (PDF evidence packs, tamper-evident log) or **Easy Withdraw** (signed PDFs).
- **Before installing:** no listing shows the exact button wording. Check in the editor that you can write "Renoncer au contrat ici" and "Confirmer la rétractation" for French customers.

Also look at what Shopify offers on its [help page on the EU right of withdrawal](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/compliance/legal/eu-right-of-withdrawal). On the [Shopify Community forum](https://community.shopify.com/t/eu-withdrawal-button-requirement-starts-june-19-2026/637624), merchants explain they picked an app because the native flow went through customer accounts with a verification code.
